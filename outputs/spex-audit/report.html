UX-flow findings

blocker dead-end 'Mark as Complete' button produces no observable response
On the order detail page (ON-00000101), Priya clicked 'Mark as Complete' at step 0, waited for a response, then tried it again at step 3 after attempting the status path as a fallback. Neither attempt produced any visible change — no toast, no modal, no status label update. The button is present and visually prominent, but the flow dead-ends there with zero feedback. Without any confirmation that the action succeeded or failed, Priya has no signal before ending the buyer call. This is the primary CTA for the JTBD and it silently does nothing.

After 'Mark as Complete' failed to respond, Priya tried the SLDS path component as a fallback (step 2: selected 'Activated'). major decision-overload Cart tab is too dense to navigate during a live call — accessibility tree truncated before a submission action could be found
When Priya pivoted to the Cart (5) tab at step 4 looking for a 'Place Order' or 'Submit' affordance, the accessibility tree was truncated before any completion CTA could be identified, leaving the persona stuck (step 5). A page dense enough to overflow the accessibility tree will equally overwhelm a rep who is managing a buyer on the phone and needs to confirm an action in seconds. The Cart view needs a clear focal hierarchy: a single prominent 'Confirm Order' or 'Place Order' CTA anchored at the top of the panel, above the line-item list — not buried at the end of a long scroll.

major missing-affordance Seven context-bar icon buttons have no discernible label — navigation actions are unidentifiable
The context bar (.slds-context-bar__item) contains 7 icon-only buttons with no accessible name, flagged as critical axe violations. A rep scanning the bar for navigation options — 'how do I get back to the order list', 'how do I open related records' — cannot identify these actions by sight or assistive technology. During a time-pressured call, unlabeled icons force the rep to stop and hover-test each one. Add text labels or persistent visible tooltips to all icon-only context bar actions. Note: the visual execution (icon contrast, sizing) falls under V4/V6 — this finding is about the missing navigational affordance.
